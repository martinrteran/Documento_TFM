\section*{Abstract}
\par\end{center}

This work studies reactive map-less navigation of an automated guided
vehicle (AGV) on a two-dimensional grid with static obstacles using deep
reinforcement learning. The problem is formulated as a partially
observable Markov decision process (POMDP): the agent does not receive
the map, but a local observation of $18$ components made up of eight
sectors of a simulated LiDAR and the distance and bearing to the goal.
An original composite reward function is proposed, combining progress
towards the goal, a per-step cost, an obstacle-proximity penalty and terminal terms,
and value-based agents (DQN and Double DQN) and actor-critic agents
(PPO, in a buffer-based variant and in the standard implementation) are
trained and compared on four maps of increasing difficulty.

Across seventeen experimental configurations, six of them trained with
five seeds, two failure modes of the reactive policy are identified and
each one is addressed with a specific remedy. Collisions next to thin
walls, which capped the success rate on the hardest map at about
$78\%$, are removed by masking invalid actions. Loops between cells with
almost identical observations, a direct consequence of partial
observability, drop from $42.6$ to $6.2$ per $1\,000$ episodes when the
robot's last displacement is appended to the observation, a one-step
implicit memory that proves more effective than a GRU recurrent network.
With both mechanisms, PPO reaches a $99.4 \pm 0.4\%$ success rate under
deterministic evaluation, against $87.9 \pm 3.8\%$ for Double DQN under
the same conditions, with near-optimal routes and a seed-to-seed spread
about ten times smaller. The results are restricted to a discrete
four-action kinematics and static maps, and the action mask relies on
privileged information from the environment; generalisation to unseen
maps, continuous kinematics and transfer to a real robot are left as
future work.
